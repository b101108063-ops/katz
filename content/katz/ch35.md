<span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> SELECTIVE INHIBITORS
<span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> selective inhibitors, or coxibs, were developed in an attempt
to inhibit prostaglandin synthesis by the <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> isozyme induced
at sites of inflammation without affecting the action of the consti-
tutively active “housekeeping” <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-1</span> isozyme found in the GI
tract, kidneys, and platelets. Coxibs selectively bind to and block the
active site of the <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> enzyme much more effectively than that of
<span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-1</span>. <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> inhibitors have analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-in-
flammatory effects similar to those of non-selective <span style="color:#60a5fa">NSAID</span> but
with an approximate halving of GI adverse effects. Likewise, <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span>
inhibitors at usual doses have no impact on platelet aggregation,
which is mediated by thromboxane produced by the <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-1</span>
isozyme. In contrast, they do inhibit <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span>-mediated prostacyclin
synthesis in the vascular endothelium. As a result, <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> inhibitors
do not offer the cardioprotective effects of traditional nonselective
<span style="color:#60a5fa">NSAID</span>, which has resulted in some patients taking low-dose
<span style="color:#fb923c">aspirin</span> in addition to a coxib regimen to maintain this effect.
Unfortunately, because <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> is constitutively active within the
kidney, recommended doses of <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> inhibitors cause renal toxici-
ties similar to those associated with traditional <span style="color:#60a5fa">NSAID</span>. Clinical
data have suggested a higher incidence of cardiovascular thrombotic
events associated with <span style="color:#60a5fa">COX-2</span> inhibitors such as <span style="color:#fb923c">rofecoxib</span> and val-
decoxib, resulting in their withdrawal from the market.
