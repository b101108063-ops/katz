## 章節導讀

本章節是抗菌藥物的總論，介紹抗菌化學治療的基本原則。

**選擇性毒性概念**
- 抗菌藥物作用於微生物特有的靶點，對宿主細胞相對無害
- 主要靶點：細胞壁合成、蛋白質合成、核酸合成、細胞膜功能

**抗菌藥物分類（依作用機轉）**
- **抑制細胞壁合成**：β-lactams（Penicillins、Cephalosporins、Carbapenems）、Vancomycin
- **抑制蛋白質合成**：Aminoglycosides（30S）、Tetracyclines（30S）、Macrolides（50S）
- **抑制核酸合成**：Quinolones（DNA gyrase）、Rifampin（RNA polymerase）

**抗藥性機轉**
- 產生酶使藥物去活化（如 β-lactamase）
- 改變藥物靶點結構（如 MRSA 的 PBP2a）
- 減少藥物滲透或增加外流

**抗菌藥物使用原則**
- 確診或高度懷疑細菌感染才使用
- 依據感染部位、病原菌、病人因素選擇藥物

## INTRODUCTION TO ANTIMICROBIAL

AGENTS
<span style="color:#60a5fa">Antimicrobial</span> agents provide some of the most dramatic examples
of the advances of modern medicine. Many infectious diseases once
considered incurable and lethal are now amenable to treatment
with a few pills. The remarkably<span style="color:#60a5fa">antimicrobial</span> specific activity of
antimicrobial drugs is due to their selectivity for targets that are
either unique to prokaryote and fungal microorganisms or much
more important in these organisms than in humans. Among these
targets are bacterial and fungal <span style="color:#22d3ee">cell wall</span>-synthesizing enzymes
( Chapters 43 and  48 ), the bacterial ribosome ( Chapters 44 and
45 ), the enzymes required for nucleotide synthesis and DNA rep-
lication ( Chapter 46 ), and the machinery of viral replication
( Chapter 49 ). The special group of drugs used in mycobacterial
infections is discussed in  Chapter 47 . The much older and less
selective cytotoxic antiseptics and disinfectants are discussed in
Chapter 50 . The clinical uses of all these agents are summarized in
Chapter 51 .
The major problem threatening the continued success of anti-
microbial drugs is the development of <span style="color:#f87171">resistant</span> organisms.
Microorganisms can adapt to environm<span style="color:#60a5fa">antibiotic</span>sures in a variety
of<span style="color:#60a5fa">antimicrobial</span>ys, and their response to antibiotic pressure is no
exception. An inevitable consequence of antim<span style="color:#f87171">resistant</span>usage is the
selection of resistant microorganisms, perhaps the most obvious
example of evolution in action. Overuse and inappropriate use of
antibioti<span style="color:#f87171">resistant</span>ients has fueled a major increase in prevalence
of multidrug-resistant pathogens. Antibacterial antibiotics are
misused by providers in a variety of ways, including use in patients
who are unlikely to have bacterial infections, use over unnecessar-
ily prolonged periods, and use <span style="color:#f87171">resistant</span>le agents or broad-
spectrum agents when not needed.
As a result of antibiotic pressure, highly resistant gram-negative
organisms with novel mechanisms of resistance are increasingly
reported. Some of these strains have spread over vast geographic
areas as a result of patients seeking medical care in different coun-
tries. Much larger quantities of antibiotics have been used in
agriculture to stimulate growth and prevent infection in farm
animals and this has added to the selection pressure that results in
resistant organisms.
Unfortunately, as the need has grown in recent years, develop-
ment of novel drugs has slowed. The most vulnerable molecular
targets of antimicrobial drugs have been identified and, in many
cases, crystallized and characterized. Pending the identification of
new targets and compounds, it seems likely that over the next decade
we will have to rely on currently available families of drugs. In the
face of continuing development of resistance, considerable effort will
be required to maintain the effectiveness of these drug groups.
